AlgoGlass

A playable composition after the structural ideas of Philip Glass: repetition, additive phrases that grow and shrink a note at a time, rhythmic cycles of 5, 7 and 11 eighths drifting against one another, slow harmonic change and deliberate silence. A seeded generator (1976) writes sixteen digits, plays them four times, then changes two of them. Each digit moves the harmony one chord along F minor → D♭ → A → B7 and sets the chord's length, its arpeggio and its inversion. The music and the picture are drawn from the same events, so what you see is what you hear.

The voice sings the sixteen digits as they come round, as the chorus counts and sings solfège in Einstein on the Beach. Knee plays turn the end of each movement into a spare interlude of organ and counting voice. The sound engineer rides the mix, as Kurt Munkacsi did live with the Glass Ensemble.
