Algogenesis · Panel I

A living world for a wall

A coast where flowers and animals evolve without a script: bodies, colours, patterns and light are all grown from genes, and they change only when evolution pays for the change. This is the first panel of an installation, running in the cloud for now; the same program is built to run on a computer behind a 4' × 6' screen.

The journal

At the end of every epoch (12 world days) a language model writes one entry from the facts the world measured. It describes; it never decides what evolves.

The first entries appear as the world ages.

What is evolving

How the world works

  • A coast with tides of years. Deep water, shallows, beaches and meadows. The sea rises and falls slowly, moving the shore and the flowers with it.
  • Flowers need animals. A flower sets seed only when an animal brings pollen from another flower of its own kind. Animals drink nectar and are drawn to the colours they prefer, so flowers and their pollinators evolve together.
  • Bodies are grown from genes: segments, pairs of legs, size, a tail, a crest of spines, fins or feet, two colours laid out as stripes or spots, and the ability to glow. A diet gene runs from nectar to meat.
  • Beauty has a cost and a reason. Mates are chosen by colour and display, and only an animal in good condition can afford a bright one, so choosy parents have healthier young. The same brightness makes an animal easier for a hunter to find. By day colour shows; at night only light does.
  • No single winner. Each species carries its own enemies, which multiply as it spreads, so a runaway success is held back before the world becomes a monoculture.
  • Refugia. The world keeps a bank of past lineages. If animals nearly vanish, banked lineages return, so a crash costs time but not everything that has evolved.
  • Families. Young are born small, slow and easy prey. A gene for care lets parents feed and guard the young that stay close, and the young follow them; on the board a dotted thread joins a youngster to its parent. Care spreads only if it pays.
  • The fossil record in the window of the tray lays down one column per epoch: the leading animals in their colours above, the leading flowers below.
  • The cabinet. The world sits behind the glass of a vintage pachinko board. All the words are behind the five keys in the tray (and the nameplate): the world now, the journal, the fossil record, families, and how it works.

From the cloud to the wall

  • Now: the world runs on Oedio's server at 20 steps a second (a day lasts two minutes) and saves itself every minute. Any browser can show it.
  • On site: the same engine runs on a small computer behind the panel with python -m darwin.algogenesis.standalone. It needs only Python, numpy and scipy, keeps its world and journal in a folder, survives power cuts, and serves the panel to a full-screen browser.
  • The journal on site uses a local language model through any OpenAI-compatible server (for example Ollama on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin or a small PC with a graphics card), so the installation needs no internet.
  • Several panels: each starts from its own seed and becomes its own world. Panels on the same network can trade a few migrants every few epochs, a few genomes at a time (set ALGO_PEERS), so they diverge like islands and occasionally exchange travellers.
  • Display: a 4' × 6' screen is 3:2; the panel letterboxes to fit any screen. Use LCD or LED rather than OLED for a picture that runs day and night.
